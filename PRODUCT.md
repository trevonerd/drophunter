# Product

## Register

product

## Users

DropHunter is for Twitch viewers who actively collect Drops and want the farming workflow to stay organized without manually juggling campaign pages, eligible stream tabs, claim states, queue order, and browser restarts. Users are often multitasking while streams run in the background, then returning to the popup or monitor for quick status checks.

Their primary job is to choose one or more Twitch Drops campaigns, start farming, understand whether progress is moving, and recover quickly when Twitch, playback, session state, or campaign availability changes.

## Product Purpose

DropHunter is a Chrome, Chromium, and Edge extension that automates and monitors Twitch Drops farming on twitch.tv. It detects available campaigns, lets users queue campaigns, opens eligible streams, tracks progress, claims rewards when possible, rotates streamers only when needed, and preserves session progress across common browser lifecycle interruptions.

Success means the user can trust the extension to do the repetitive work locally, see exactly what is happening at a glance, and intervene only when Twitch requires human attention.

## Brand Personality

DropHunter should feel focused, capable, and quietly game-adjacent. The tone is practical and transparent: confident about automation, honest about Twitch-controlled limitations, and respectful of the user's attention.

The interface should feel like a compact control surface for an ongoing task, not a promotional landing page or a decorative dashboard. It can borrow energy from Twitch's visual world, but should prioritize clarity, state legibility, and fast repeated use.

## Anti-references

Do not make DropHunter look like a marketing site, growth dashboard, ad-tech tool, crypto tracker, or generic SaaS analytics product.

Avoid decorative metrics, oversized hero treatments, generic card grids, fake depth, ornamental glass effects, and interfaces that hide operational state behind vibes. Avoid designs that imply DropHunter owns Twitch campaign data or guarantees progress when Twitch ultimately controls eligibility, claimability, and playback behavior.

## Design Principles

1. Make the current farming state impossible to miss. Running, paused, recovering, blocked, stale, complete, and sign-in-required states should each have clear visual and textual affordances.
2. Preserve campaign identity. Campaigns are not plain games, so selection, queue, labels, and completion feedback must stay campaign-aware.
3. Be calm until action is needed. Routine progress should be compact and readable; warnings should escalate only when user attention genuinely matters.
4. Show local trust. Reinforce that DropHunter works only with the user's local browser session and Twitch endpoints, with no analytics or developer-owned backend.
5. Respect extension ergonomics. The popup and monitor are small, repeated-use tools; layout, copy, controls, and motion should serve quick scanning over spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target at least WCAG 2.1 AA for contrast, focus visibility, labels, status messaging, and keyboard access within the popup and monitor. State changes should use screen-reader friendly status regions where relevant. Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences, especially loading, onboarding, and progress animations. Color should never be the only carrier of status because users may be monitoring progress peripherally, under poor display conditions, or with color-vision differences.
